# 2.3 Review of Related and Existing Systems

This section reviews research-developed systems and commercial platforms related to decision support, fitness personalization, body-composition assessment, interpretation, and progress tracking. The research systems are described using their original peer-reviewed publications. Features of the commercial systems are based on official vendor documentation and are presented only as documented product capabilities, not as independent evidence of their accuracy, usability, or effectiveness.

**Prioritize, Personalize, Prescribe Exercise (P3-EX) [3]:** Pescatello et al. developed P3-EX as a clinical decision-support system for patients with multiple cardiovascular disease risk factors. It combines pre-participation screening, cardiovascular-health scoring, established exercise-prescription strategies, and the frequency, intensity, time, and type principle to prioritize risk factors and support an individualized exercise prescription. The system shows how structured information and documented decision logic can be used to prioritize personal health concerns. However, P3-EX is clinical and prescriptive, whereas FitStart is a non-clinical educational system that prioritizes measurements from a completed FitMao assessment. The authors also stated that further evaluation was needed to establish P3-EX's feasibility, acceptability, and clinical utility.

**Personalized Fitness Recommender System (Zhao et al.) [4]:** Zhao et al. developed a gamified fitness assistant that uses dynamic player modeling and wearable-based activity tracking to provide personalized activity recommendations. The system updates the user's model over time and adapts its recommendations and game features to the user's context. It was evaluated in a 60-day study involving 40 participants. This work supports the use of personal context in fitness personalization, but its main purpose is to encourage continued physical activity and engagement rather than to interpret or prioritize the measurements in a completed body-composition report.

**FitMao-280 [13]:** FitMao-280 is the body-composition analyzer that supplies the assessment information used by FitStart. Its official documentation describes an eight-electrode Direct Segmental Multi-frequency Bio-impedance Analysis method, printed reports, personal health records, historical result viewing, body-composition change curves, and more than 40 data items. These include body weight, body-fat percentage, skeletal muscle measurements, BMI, visceral fat, body water, basal metabolic rate, target weight, and control values. FitMao produces and presents the measurements, while FitStart uses the member-confirmed values as input for a separate, non-clinical interpretation and prioritization process. FitStart does not generate, correct, or medically validate the measurements produced by FitMao.

**InBody App [30]:** InBody App is a companion application for InBody body-composition analyzers. Official documentation describes access to body-composition results, personalized insights, result-sheet breakdowns, comparison of results over time, and custom diet and exercise plans. InBody therefore provides more than simple storage or display of raw measurements. However, the official materials reviewed do not document a FitMao-specific workflow or the same visible, deterministic, per-metric scoring trace used by FitStart to explain how a Main Focus and additional priorities were selected.

**ACCUNIQ Connect [33]:** ACCUNIQ Connect is a mobile application for users of ACCUNIQ body-composition analyzers. It transfers assessment results by scanning a QR code from the analyzer screen or printed report, stores measurements on the user's device, displays changes in weight, muscle mass, and body-fat percentage, and provides goals and calorie guidance. Its official documentation also describes a physical-activity preparation questionnaire that uses health responses and measurement data, together with activity level, target diet period, and control target. ACCUNIQ Connect is therefore one of the closest systems to FitStart in terms of QR-based transfer, contextual input, saving, and comparison. The public documentation reviewed, however, does not describe the same Main Focus and Top Priorities structure or an itemized rule trace showing how each response changed the priority score of each measurement.

**Evolt Active [34]:** Evolt Active is a companion application for the Evolt body-composition ecosystem. The vendor describes centralized scan records, progress tracking, personalized body-scan insights, goal tracking, interactive health questionnaires, a proprietary Bio Wellness Index score, macronutrient profiles, supplement suggestions, and support for training and nutrition planning. It is relevant because it combines body-composition information with broader wellness guidance. Unlike FitStart, however, Evolt Active extends into program, nutrition, and supplement guidance, while FitStart is intentionally limited to explaining and prioritizing FitMao measurements without generating a workout, diet, supplement, or treatment plan.

**seca myAnalytics [35]:** seca myAnalytics presents results from compatible seca body-composition devices, explains individual parameters, and displays changes across repeated measurements. Current vendor documentation also describes an optional feature called AI Health Insights, which provides an initial personalized assessment and recommendations before a professional consultation. This makes seca myAnalytics relevant to result interpretation and longitudinal review. Its approach differs from FitStart because FitStart does not use artificial intelligence; it uses fixed and documented rules and exposes the scoring adjustments behind each personalized priority.

**Synthesis:** The reviewed systems show that body-composition results can be stored, explained, compared over time, and connected with goals, questionnaires, or recommendations. FitStart should therefore not be presented as the first or only system to interpret or personalize body-composition information. Its narrower contribution is the particular combination implemented for newly registered gym members: FitMao QR scanning or uploaded-image input, member verification of the captured measurements, questionnaire-based context, deterministic metric-level prioritization, and a visible explanation of how specific measurements and responses produced a Main Focus and two additional Top Priorities. This conclusion applies only to the studies and publicly documented systems reviewed and does not claim that no comparable system exists elsewhere.
